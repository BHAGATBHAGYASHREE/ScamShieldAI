Deception Mechanics

Anatomy of the Deception

Why social engineering attacks bypass human intuition: psychological triggers over brute force.

⏱️

Manufactured Urgency

“Immediately”, “within 24 hours”, “by mistake” create artificial time pressure that causes cognitive overload, preventing deliberate verification.

🎭

Social Engineering

Scammers exploit politeness and empathy. Framing themselves as an innocent victim who “made a mistake” tricks targets into acting without checking account balances.

🖼️

The Illusion of Proof

A forged payment screenshot mimics authentic bank typography, creating visual reassurance before the target opens their own banking portal.

The Key Takeaway: The attack wasn't technically sophisticated. It exploited human cognitive biases: urgency, emotional empathy, and manufactured visual proof.

Feature Engineering Architecture

The Engineering Engine Inside features.py

Scikit-Learn compliant TransformerMixin, precompiled high-speed regexes, and mathematical Shannon character entropy.

⚙️ Custom Scikit-Learn Transformer Architecture

class ScamFeatureExtractor(BaseEstimator, TransformerMixin): def fit(self, X, y=None): return self # Stateless feature extraction def transform(self, X) -> np.ndarray: # Extracts 18 domain signals in parallel (<0.1ms per item) features = [extract_features_single(m) for m in X] return np.array([[f[col] for col in FEATURE_NAMES] for f in features])
Critical Serialization Fix (The Joblib Pickle Bug)

If defined in a Jupyter notebook, custom transformers serialize into module __main__, triggering AttributeError: Can't get attribute 'ScamFeatureExtractor' during API unpickling. Packaging it into a standalone features.py guarantees seamless unpickling in FastAPI & pytest.

📐 Shannon Character Entropy Formula

H(X) = - ∑ P(xi) · log2 P(xi)

Why it matters: Obfuscated malicious links (e.g. bit.ly/x7Q9zP_1) and scrambled phone strings possess abnormally high character entropy compared to standard natural English text.

🔗 Scikit-Learn FeatureUnion Fusion

pipeline = Pipeline([ ('features', FeatureUnion([ ('tfidf', TfidfVectorizer(ngram_range=(1,2), max_features=8000)), ('heuristics', Pipeline([ ('extractor', ScamFeatureExtractor()), ('scaler', StandardScaler()) ])) ])), ('clf', CalibratedClassifierCV(estimator, cv=3)) ])

Architectural Defense

Why This Architecture Over Obvious Alternatives?

Every design choice addresses latency, cloud hosting expenses, feature obfuscation, and trust.

DECISION 01

Why Not Deep Learning / BERT (DeBERTa)?

• BERT requires an expensive GPU cluster in production, has 100M+ parameters, and introduces 200–500ms latency.

• ScamShield runs on standard low-cost CPU containers with < 1ms inference latency (0.08ms), $0 GPU bills, and complete token-level explainability.

DECISION 02

Why Not Standard CountVectorizer?

• Scammers deliberately obfuscate words (e.g. bit.ly, Rs. 14,999, ALL-CAPS urgency).

• CountVectorizer treats tokens in isolation and completely ignores character entropy, URL redirects, and regex syntax ratios.

DECISION 03

Why Probability Calibration?

• Raw SVM or logistic margins output uncalibrated heuristic scores that distort real risks.

• CalibratedClassifierCV (Platt scaling with 3-fold CV) ensures a 90% risk score truly reflects a 9-in-10 probability of real fraud.

Decision 04: Linear Hyperplane vs Heavy Trees on Sparse Text

With 8,000+ sparse TF-IDF features, linear classifiers find convex separating hyperplanes in 0.08ms without the exponential depth overhead and greedy overfitting of decision tree splits.
